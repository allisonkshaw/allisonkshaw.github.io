My research aims to understand the causes and consequences of long-distance movement, and how this intersects with both interspecific interactions (especially mutualism, parasitism, and pathogens) and spatial patterns. You can read more about my lab community and the research interests of lab members (which often diverge from mine).

I believe that fostering a diversity of voices in science is both the just thing to do and leads to better science overall (read [my thoughts on the role of diversity for theory in biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12080-022-00533-1)).

I am committed to providing a safe and equitable lab environment; a commitment I realize in part through [clear communication of lab expectations](https://www.dropbox.com/s/f35gmvhhc3h8gfy/ShawLab%20Expectations%20web.docx?dl=0) and [regular self-assessments](https://www.dropbox.com/s/3gh8dljxgfuc02r/ShawLab%20self-assessment.docx?dl=0).

Read our [lab's theory persona framework](https://allisonkshaw.weebly.com/personas.html).
